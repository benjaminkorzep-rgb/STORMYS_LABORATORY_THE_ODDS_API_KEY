10The case against the verdict

The strongest argument for Denver, and what would change my mind.

The case for Denver: Boston's 11-3 record is built on a soft early schedule: Charlotte, Chicago twice, Brooklyn, Dallas, and a Toronto team missing Barnes. Its team field-goal percentage (42.6%) and three-point percentage (about 33%) are poor. It is winning on volume and defense, which swings more from night to night than shot-making. Denver has the best player on the floor, at home, at altitude, against a team flying in for the first game of a road trip. Boston has no settled center. The 16 Nov loss showed the weakness: Garza started, Robinson picked up 5 fouls in 18 min, and Orlando won the big-man minutes with Bitadze +14. Denver's starting five keeps the full lineup on the floor for 34–39 min. Its weak net rating is mostly a bench problem, and the bench plays the fewest minutes in a close game.

Best evidence: Jokić is averaging 24.1/10.9/8.9. Boston's center spot has had three different starters. Denver gets to the line more (FT rate 8th vs Boston's 24th), which is exactly what punishes a foul-prone Boston frontcourt.

What would change my mind: (1) Boston starts Garza or Robinson because Queta is unavailable. (2) George or Tatum is ruled out. (3) The market moves to Denver −1 or better without news. Any one of these moves the verdict to Denver 50–58%. This case is why the range has a 50% floor and the confidence is Medium, not High.

12Product and scale decision

One user, one decision, one game a night. The budget cannot license all 30 teams.

Backtest method

  • Starters: freeze Section 5 at T−60. Compare it with the posted starters at T−30 and with the box-score starters. Score each slot as right or wrong, and log which signal drove each miss.
  • Minutes: compare each projected range with actual minutes. Report the error by role (star, starter, bench) and flag blowout or overtime games separately, so outliers do not hide method errors.
  • Matchups: score the "primary defender" call against NBA.com matchup tracking (the defender with the most partial possessions). Target ≥ 60% correct. This is tracked, not gated, in week one.
  • Win ranges: log the range and the outcome. Seven games cannot test calibration, so this is recorded for later and not gated.
  • Pre-launch replay: rebuild starter calls for 30 past games from archived injury-report PDFs at their T−60 versions. That shows whether the method clears 90% before going live.
Go / no-go: if all four gates pass, expand to 3 games per night, not all games, and re-gate. If any gate fails: no-go. Fix the failing step and run another 7 nights. If the same gate fails twice, stop and change the product, for example to a starters-only alert.
